# Methods

Written to be lifted into a paper or use-case write-up. Every number quoted here
is regenerated by `./reproduce.sh` and written to `results/`.

## 1. Question

Does the choice of molecular featurizer matter for ADMET prediction, and does the
answer depend on how the data is split? Secondarily: do reported ranking
differences between featurizers survive the uncertainty they are quoted with?

The study is deliberately designed so that its headline claim can fail, and in
the course of this work it did (see §7).

## 2. Data

Two public, permissively licensed sources, retrieved by `src/data.py`:

- **ESOL / Delaney**: aqueous solubility, regression, 1,128 compounds
- **Tox21**: twelve binary toxicity assays, ~8,000 compounds; NR-AR, SR-MMP and
  NR-AhR are used here (7,432 / 5,920 / 6,691 compounds after parsing)

Both are distributed with the DeepChem repository under the MIT licence. SMILES
that RDKit cannot parse are dropped rather than imputed; no compound is otherwise
excluded.

`src/data.py:load_internal` accepts a private assay export in the same shape,
requiring SMILES, a measured value, and an assay run date.

## 3. Featurizers

| Arm | Representation | Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| `morgan` | Morgan/ECFP4 fingerprint, radius 2 | 2,048 |
| `descriptors` | RDKit 2D physicochemical descriptors | 217 |
| `combo` | concatenation of the above | 2,265 |
| `chemberta` | mean-pooled ChemBERTa-77M-MTR hidden states | 384 |

`morgan` is designated the baseline. A small number of RDKit descriptors (Ipc in
particular) exceed float32 range and are clipped rather than dropped.

The `chemberta` arm requires `torch` and `transformers` and was not exercised in
the results below; it registers automatically when those are installed and is
subject to the same gate.

## 4. Splits

| Split | Construction |
|---|---|
| `random` | uniform permutation, 80/20 |
| `scaffold` | Bemis–Murcko scaffolds grouped, largest groups assigned to train until 80% is reached, remainder to test |
| `temporal` | ordered by assay run date, first 80% train |

Scaffold assignment is deterministic by construction. `temporal` requires a date
column; public benchmark sets carry none, so it raises an explanatory error
rather than silently falling back. The absence of that column is the finding it
is there to surface.

## 5. Model

`HistGradientBoostingRegressor` / `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`
(scikit-learn), 300 iterations, early stopping enabled, otherwise default
hyperparameters. Hyperparameters are held constant across arms: the study
compares representations, not tuning budgets, and giving one arm a tuning
advantage would confound exactly the comparison being made.

## 6. Metrics and uncertainty

Regression: RMSE (primary), R². Classification: ROC-AUC (primary), PR-AUC.

Two sources of variance are quantified separately (`src/experiments.py`):

- **Test-set variance**: percentile bootstrap over the held-out set, 500 (the value `reproduce.sh` passes; the code default is 1,000)
  resamples, 95% interval. Resamples that lose a class are skipped, the standard
  handling for AUC.
- **Model-seed variance**: the fit is repeated across five seeds and the
  standard deviation reported. For the random split the seed also varies the
  split; for the scaffold split the partition is fixed and the seed varies the
  fit alone.

## 7. Results

set.

| | descriptors | 0.586 | 0.938 | +0.352 |
| | combo | 0.584 | 0.928 | +0.343 |
| tox21:NR-AR (AUC ↑) | morgan | 0.801 | 0.731 | −0.070 |

| | combo | 0.769 | 0.735 | −0.034 |

Bootstrap intervals for every cell are in `results/with_intervals.csv`.

### 7.1 The split shift holds up

Scaffold splitting costs something in all twelve endpoint–arm combinations: 0.018
to 0.108 ROC-AUC on the classification endpoints, and 48% to 60% added RMSE on
ESOL. This is the finding the study supports.

The direction holds everywhere. The magnitude does not, and the random and
scaffold bootstrap intervals fail to overlap in nine of the twelve combinations,
not all of them. The three exceptions are the three NR-AR arms, which have the
smallest shifts and the widest intervals in the study.

### 7.2 The ranking claim did not survive

An earlier single-seed run of this work reported that the winning arm changed
with the split on three of four endpoints, and that finding was written into the
project's documentation. Repeating across five seeds reduced it to **one of
four** (`tox21:NR-AR`).

Which one survives is the informative part. NR-AR is also the only endpoint where
all three arms sit inside each other's bootstrap intervals, under both splits: its
intervals are roughly 0.15 ROC-AUC wide against arm-to-arm differences of 0.005 to
0.037. On ESOL and SR-MMP, where `descriptors` and `combo` are cleanly separated
from `morgan`, the winner does not change at all.

The reordering therefore appears exactly where the arms cannot be told apart, and
nowhere else. That is single-seed noise between statistically indistinguishable
arms rather than a property of the splits, and the claim is withdrawn. `results/claim_check.json`
records the test, including the criterion under which it fails.

The weaker surviving statement is more useful: where arms sit inside each other's
intervals, "which featurizer is best" is not a well-posed question, and a
leaderboard answering it reports noise. This strengthens rather than weakens the
argument for a per-endpoint gate.

### 7.3 Gate

An arm ships for an endpoint only if it beats `morgan` under the hardest
available split for that endpoint, evaluated independently per endpoint
(`out/gate.json`). Where intervals overlap, the cheaper and more
interpretable arm is preferred.

## 8. Limitations

1. `temporal` is never exercised, because no public benchmark carries assay
   dates. The split most predictive of deployment behaviour is therefore
   untested here, and the scaffold split is a proxy for it.
2. No repeatability ceiling. Public benchmarks carry single measurements, so the
   assay-reproducibility bound on achievable performance cannot be computed.
   On internal data it can, and it should be, before any target metric is agreed.
3. Fixed hyperparameters. A tuned `morgan` might close part of its gap; the
   comparison is between representations at equal tuning budget.
4. Three endpoints from one assay panel plus one solubility set. Conclusions
   about "endpoints in general" are not supported.
5. `chemberta` untested in these results.

## 9. Reproduction

```
./reproduce.sh          # Linux / macOS
.\reproduce.ps1         # Windows
```

Downloads the public datasets, runs the gauntlet, then the repeated-seed and
bootstrap analysis, writing all reported numbers to `results/`. Approximately
15 minutes on a laptop; no GPU required.
